## How we read the completion evidence

The deliverable is a checklist **with unresolved decisions explicitly marked** — so it is designed to be completed while most answers are still missing. A checklist that waited for every answer would never be delivered at all. What makes it complete is that every requirement is enumerated and every one carries a status, not that every status says resolved.

The constraint splits the work in two. Statuses are public; content is not.

## Sub-task 1 — Profiles and consent

### Results and analysis

Nothing here can be resolved by Dingyi, and all of it is enumerated rather than left vague: OpenReview profile completeness, submission quota, final author set, contribution conditions and agreement to submit — five requirements, all unresolved, for all three authors.

Two of them bite harder than they look:

1. **The author set cannot change after submission.** TMLR permits no addition or removal once submitted. So the pending order and consent in DK-02 are not paperwork to tidy up afterwards; they are a gate.
2. **Submission quota is per author.** TMLR limits how many submissions an author may have under review at once. If Yang or Xue is at quota, the submission cannot proceed regardless of everything else. Nobody has checked.

**"Ask all authors" is an action, and it has not happened.** The clause begins with a verb. Enumerating the questions is not the same as putting them to the authors, and nothing has been sent — the owner has been messaged about DK-01 only. Until that message goes out, these rows cannot begin to move.

**Conflicting archival publications: checked, with one finding.** This work is **not** on arXiv — searched by title, by the earlier "Harness Intelligence Level" title used in v2.4, and by author. Two things did surface:

1. **A prepared arXiv submission that was never made.** `paper-sources/unified-theory/ARXIV.md` is a complete submission guide for v2.4: metadata, categories, checklist. It lists **two** authors, uses the older "Harness Intelligence Level" title, and leaves the licence unchosen with the note that "the author must choose this in the submission form". Whether it is still intended matters for the concurrent-submission question.
2. **An overlapping arXiv paper by an author that this manuscript does not cite.** "Self-Aware Recursively Self-Improving Agents for Personal Singularity" (arXiv:2607.12254, 2026-07-14, Chengshuai Yang) defines a persistent self-model of identity, goals, capabilities, limitations and developmental change, used to govern recursive self-improvement. That overlaps this paper's Self-Awareness family and its I3–I5 self-improvement ladder directly, and it appears **nowhere** in `references.bib` or `main.tex`. Omitting the closest prior work, by one's own author, is the kind of gap a reviewer finds and reads badly. Third-person self-citation is permitted under double-blind review.

**Prior publication.** Versions 2.0 and 2.6 of this work are publicly posted under the authors' names, and a companion site hosts them. TMLR explicitly permits preprints — but it also requires that the submission not be linked to an identified version. The relevant risk is recorded in DK-07: the evidence archive still describes itself by the v2.0 title, which is searchable back to the authors.

## Sub-task 2 — Declarations and rights

### Results and analysis

Funding, competing interests and conflicts are all unknown to Dingyi and are recorded as questions rather than guesses.

**One competing-interest question is worth naming explicitly**, because it will not occur to anyone to declare it: NextGen PlatformAI C Corp is both the affiliation of two authors and the operator of the public site that hosts this work and its benchmark. Whether TMLR considers that a competing interest is the authors' judgement, but leaving it undeclared by default is not a neutral choice.

**Human subjects: likely not applicable.** Both studies measure model–harness pairs, and no human participant data appears anywhere in the released evidence. Recorded as *likely n/a, needs confirmation* rather than resolved, because an author should confirm it instead of us inferring it.

**Action editors: six candidates, verified against the published board.** The first version of this list came from a summarized page fetch, which is not good enough when the output is six real people's names and affiliations going to the owner. Re-read from the board itself and searched exhaustively across all **705** listed action editors: exactly two mention *benchmark*, two mention *llm agent*, one mentions *reproducibility*, and none mentions *evaluation*. The five originally listed all check out — and the search turned up one that had been missed, **Olawale Elijah Salaudeen (Microsoft)**, whose expertise reads *"benchmarking, … validity"*. Given that §17 of this paper is "The Validity of the Items", that is arguably the best fit on the board. One correction in the other direction: Andrew Lampinen's relevance is memory and cognition, not evaluation, so he is a different-angle candidate rather than a close match.

**Action editors: originally five candidates drafted.** Previously deferred entirely on the grounds that the author set and conflicts come first. That was half right — a conflict disqualifies an editor, so the final choice does need the conflict lists, but topic fit can be assessed now. From TMLR's board of 400+, matched on listed expertise: Chinmay Hegde (NYU, llm benchmarking) and Yali Du (KCL, llm agents) are the closest, with Junpei Komiyama (MBZUAI, reproducibility and hypothesis testing) a strong third given how much of this paper turns on exact tests. Full table in the checklist.

**Broader impact: recommended even though not strictly required.** TMLR requires the statement only where the work "carries a significant risk of harm". This paper proposes a scale for measuring machine intelligence, which invites being quoted as a capability claim about named systems — a foreseeable misreading that a short statement could forestall. The judgement is the authors'.

**The repository has no licence at all.** Verified rather than assumed: there is no `LICENSE`, `LICENSE.md` or `COPYING` anywhere in it. With no licence, the default is all rights reserved — which is the *root* of DK-07's rights gate rather than a separate problem. DK-07 could not verify rights to the evidence archive because nothing in this repository grants any. Adding a licence is the owner's decision and a legal one; it is recorded here so it is made deliberately rather than by omission.

**Rights are the same gate as DK-07.** CC BY 4.0 applies from submission onward, not from acceptance. Approval is needed for the manuscript, for the evidence archive in the supplement, and for the inherited third-party benchmark archive inside it. None has been given. The repository's own licence is untouched.

## Sub-task 3 — Disclosure and originality

### Results and analysis

**The disclosure is drafted, built and checked, not just written.** TMLR's FAQ requires that authors who used LLM tools say so "as a footnote on the first page", and states that "the ideas, claims and results found in submissions are human-sourced, not produced by such tools". The draft in `llm_assistance_disclosure.tex` is written to do both: name the assistance accurately, and draw the line where TMLR draws it.

It was inserted into the TMLR build and the page was read:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Renders as a footnote on page 1 | yes |
| LaTeX errors | 0 |
| Page count unchanged | 63 |
| Footnote marker | none — an unnumbered footnote |

The first attempt used `\footnotetext[0]`, which printed a literal **"0"** as the marker. `\thanks` was not the answer either: it attaches to an author, and this is a paper-level statement. The working form renames `\thefootnote` to empty inside a group and restores the counter afterwards.

**The draft is accurate for one author only.** It describes the assistance Dingyi Kang used and can attest to. Yang and Xue must each state their own use, or state that they used none. A first-page disclosure that silently speaks for three authors when one wrote it is precisely the kind of inaccuracy the requirement exists to prevent.

**Original research, not a survey: resolved.** TMLR stopped accepting surveys on 2026-09-01. This paper defines a framework and reports two measurement studies of its own, so it is original research.
