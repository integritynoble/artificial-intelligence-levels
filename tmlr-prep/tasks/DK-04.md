## Sub-task 1 — Theory and notation

### 1.1 How we interpret it

| Phrase | Our interpretation |
|---|---|
| "check … definitions, notation, cumulative prerequisites, derivations, assumptions and proofs for consistency" | Internal consistency: does the paper use one symbol for one thing, state each rule the same way everywhere, and do its cross-references point where they claim? Not whether the theory is *right* — that is the authors' scientific judgement, not ours. |
| "cumulative prerequisites" | The retention rules: `K` factors, the I→M gate, the GP sub-ladder, the delegation frontier. Each is checked against every place it is stated, including the dataset's machine-readable policy. |
| "distinguish definitions, conjectures and proposed tests from proved or measured results" | Classify every central claim and check the paper labels it correctly. A definition presented as a measurement is the failure this asks for. |
| Not part of this sub-task | Judging the framework's scientific merit, or recomputing numbers (DK-05). |

### 1.2 What we did

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Read the whole paper page by page (done during DK-06) and re-read the definitional sections against each other: §5.1 and Table 7, §6 and §11.4, §11.7 and Table 17, §14.1–§14.9, and both addenda. |
| 2 | Compared every retention rule with the dataset's machine-readable `cumulative_policy.json` and the GP lattice. |
| 3 | Extracted every notation variant for the quantities the paper uses most and counted which form appears where. |
| 4 | Extracted **every cross-reference**, separating `\ref`/`\eqref` (109 + 5, which cannot go stale) from the thirteen written as literal numbers, then checked each literal one against the section or equation it names. |
| 5 | Classified all seventeen central claims by status and checked the paper's own labelling of each. |
| 6 | Scanned the text for the specific overclaiming the task warns about: "natural law", "universal", "validated", "proves", "established", "guarantee". |
| 7 | Cross-checked each level's definition against **every** place the paper states it: the ladder tables (C, I, M, O, SA, T, H), the §11 schema tables, the U-gate table (Table 12), the harness-realization table (Table 19) and Appendix B's promotion tests. |
| 8 | Recomputed the derivations that can be checked from printed inputs: the geometric-versus-arithmetic example in Table 22, the AI-Level Score weights, the §14.10 worked example, and the p* = ρ/(1+ρ) break-even result. |
| 9 | Enumerated the paper's stated assumptions and checked each is declared where it does work, rather than left implicit. |

### 1.3 Results and analysis

**Cross-references.** 114 of 127 are `\ref` or `\eqref` and cannot go stale. Of the thirteen literal ones, **three are wrong**:

| Location | Says | Should be |
|---|---|---|
| Note box after Eq. (8) | "Note on Equation (1)… Equation (2) reads S_A(T,H) as non-increasing" | Equations (7) and (8); (1)–(2) are the memory and I certification laws |
| §18.4.2 | "Harness Gain does what §14.2 claims" | §15.2 — §14.2 defines HLIS's symbols and never mentions Harness Gain |
| §15.4 | "the property §14.1 says AI-Level exists to measure" | §15.1 — §14.1 introduces HLIS, not AI-Level's purpose |

**Notation.** Three quantities are written two ways each: the net surface as `S^{net}_A(T,H)` and `S_net(A, T, H ≤ h)`; the retention factor as `K_{X,<k}` and `K_{X,<n}`; the continuous relaxation as `q*_{X,k}` and `q*_{d,k}`. The delegation frontier already had two symbols (`F_A` and `DF_{h,p}`), recorded earlier as item 1.

**Cumulative prerequisites.** Consistent everywhere checked. The I→M prerequisites in Table 7 match the dataset's mapping exactly, the GP sub-ladder rule matches the lattice, and the U gate matches `cumulative_policy.json`. The one exception is the delegation frontier, whose two equations state the non-cumulative form while the note, the gate factor and the addendum state the cumulative one (item 1).

**Claim status.** Fifteen of seventeen central claims are labelled correctly, and the upper levels are repeatedly marked as defined-but-never-run. Two are not: the memory ladder is said to be "measured at M1" with no M1 evidence anywhere (item 7), and §14.2 says every achievement variable enforces lower-level retention when A_DI does not (item 5). Full table in `claim_status.md`.

**Level definitions are consistent across all four statements.** Each U gate in Table 12 matches Appendix B's promotion test for the same transition — U1→U2 with "multi-step delegation under ≤H2" against the U2 gate "T2 at ≤H2", U2→U3 with "T3 strategy construction under ≤H1" against "T3 at ≤H1", and so on to UΩ at H0. The HG mechanisms in Table 18 match the realization rows in Table 19, the M capability table matches the M-Bench witness families, and the I→M minima match the dataset's mapping. No contradiction was found in any ladder.

**Assumptions are stated where they are load-bearing.** Proposition 1 names the assumption its exactness depends on ("the grading assumption is what makes the statement exact: if success were instead defined on delivered artifacts… the invariance would not hold"). The monotonicity of S_A in T is stated and the non-monotone case is handled by taking the frontier cumulatively. The statistical comparisons state one attempt per executor per seed and no repeat-run variance. The geometric mean's zero case has its own subsection (§14.11). §15.4 volunteers that AUC and Ceiling come from the same series, so 0.90 of the composite's weight sits on two views of one quantity. No unstated assumption was found doing load-bearing work.

**Derivations check out.** Table 22's contrast recomputes exactly (a flat [0.80]×5 profile gives 0.80 by either mean; [1,1,1,1,0.10] gives 0.82 arithmetic against 0.63 geometric), the AI-Level Score weights sum to 1.0, the §14.10 worked example gives 74.3 as printed, and the minimum admissible reliability p* = ρ/(1+ρ) is exactly the break-even point of the paper's own loss model (delivering is worthwhile when pV ≥ (1−p)ρV).

**Overclaiming: none found.** Every one of the eight uses of "universal" *denies* universality ("not a universal performance ordering", "not universal model-tier ordering"). "Established" appears mostly as "not established". Two uses of "validated" say unflagged items are "unaudited rather than validated". The phrase "natural law" does not occur.

**Analysis.**
1. **The defects are clerical, not scientific.** Every problem found in this sub-task is a stale number, a duplicated symbol or a sentence that overstates what a neighbouring equation does. None reflects a mistaken result.
2. **The pattern has one cause:** thirteen cross-references are written as literal numbers. Three have already gone stale, and the manuscript has just been renumbered again in DK-03, so the remaining ten are now living on borrowed time. Converting them is the durable fix (item 13).
3. **The paper's own discipline is unusually strong on the thing this task warns about.** A framework paper proposing an intelligence scale is exactly where "established natural law" language creeps in, and it is absent; the hedges are numerous, specific and placed where a reader would otherwise overreach.

## Sub-task 2 — References and claims

### 2.1 How we interpret it

| Phrase | Our interpretation |
|---|---|
| "verify each reference against the cited work" | Against the work itself — arXiv, Crossref, the publisher's record — not against memory or a search snippet. Every field the entry asserts (title, authors, venue, volume, pages, year) is checked. |
| | It also means checking that the paper says true things *about* each work, since a correct citation attached to a false characterization is still an error. |
| "check novelty claims" | Every "first" is read in context to see whether it claims priority over the field or only over this framework. |
| "do not describe proposed levels as established natural laws or universally validated measurements" | A scan of the actual wording, reported either way. |

### 2.2 What we did

Full method and results in `reference_audit.md`. Sixteen entries were checked against the arXiv API, five against Crossref by DOI, four against the publisher's record (PMLR, ACL Anthology), one against the issuing organization, and four are standard works verified against their publisher. Each in-text characterization of a cited work was compared with that work's own abstract or description.

### 2.3 Results and analysis

**29 of 30 references are correct in every field checked.** The single error is a month: `arc2026` says April 2026, but ARC-AGI-3 was released on 25 March 2026 and its technical report published on 27 March 2026 (item 14).

**Every in-text characterization is accurate.** The levels-of-automation lineage, Klein and Amershi on directability, Lee and See on reliance, Morris et al. on staged definitions, Hendrycks et al. on psychometrically grounded domains, Perrow on irreversibility and SAE J3016 on condition-indexed autonomy — each matches the cited work. No reference is attached to a claim it does not support.

**The connection to learning-system evaluation is present but invisible where it counts.** The body uses "benchmark" 72 times and "evaluation" 17, and §18 maps eighteen contemporary benchmarks into the architecture — but the **abstract uses neither word**, and "machine learning" appears once in the whole paper. TMLR's scope is evaluation methodology for learning systems, and the abstract is what an action editor reads when assigning the submission and judging scope. One sentence in the abstract and one in §1 would fix it (item 17).

**Novelty claims are scoped to the framework**, not the field: "the first instantiation of the ladder", "the first measured harness scaling curves". Accurate in context; one three-word qualifier would remove the ambiguity for a skimming reviewer (item 15).

**Analysis.** A reference list this clean is itself evidence about the manuscript: someone checked it carefully before us. That matters for the review packet, because it lets us tell the owner the citation risk is low and concentrate his attention on the two mislabelled claims instead.

## Review pass

Re-checked this audit's findings against the sources rather than against the record. **Nothing was overturned, and nothing new was found wrong.**

| Check | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|
| The 16 references carrying arXiv identifiers | Titles fetched from the arXiv API and compared | **0 mismatches** |
| Item 14, the ARC-AGI-3 month | ARC Prize blog archive | **Confirmed.** Announced **25 March 2026**; the bib says April. An April 14 post on human performance is the likely source of the slip |
| Claim 4, "the memory ladder is measured at M1" | Every occurrence of `M1` in the candidate read in context | **Confirmed mislabelled.** The paper asserts it twice — §23 "measured only at M1 in this work" and Appendix D "the memory ladder is measured at M1" — while all seven `M1` mentions are definitional: the ladder, the witness protocol, the metric list, the I1 prerequisite and a benchmark mapping. No method, no data, no result |
| `amershi2019` | Crossref | Exact: *Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction*, CHI 2019, `10.1145/3290605.3300233` |
| `perrow1984` | Crossref | The 1984 Basic Books original is correct; Crossref indexes only the 2000 and 2011 reissues, which is why it has no identifier |

**One small addition this turns up.** `amershi2019` has a DOI, and the audit listed it among the four entries with no identifier. Adding `doi = {10.1145/3290605.3300233}` would leave three, not four, references resting on publisher records — a one-line improvement, recorded as a proposal rather than applied.

## Not done / limitations

- Whether the framework is *scientifically correct* is not assessed here, and is not ours to assess. This audit covers internal consistency, labelling and citation accuracy.
- Claim 4 (M1) and claim 8 (A_DI retention) are recorded as mislabelled, not resolved; both need the owner.
- The references without identifiers were checked against publisher records rather than the documents themselves. The review pass found `amershi2019` **does** have a DOI (proposal above), leaving SimpleQA, Perrow and SAE J3016. Perrow is a 1984 book whose original edition Crossref does not index, and SAE J3016 is a paywalled standard, so neither is likely to gain one.
