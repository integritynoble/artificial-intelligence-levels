## Sub-task 1 — Trace the publication-note issues to the paper's claims

### 1.1 How we interpret it

| Phrase | Our interpretation |
|---|---|
| "each relevant issue in the publication notes" | The 7 numbered issues under "Known issues retained from upstream" in `PUBLICATION_NOTES.md`. All 7 were examined; "relevant" is the **outcome** of tracing, not assumed in advance. |
| "the paper's claims" | Everything the theory v2.6 PDF asserts: **(a) measured results**, **(b) definitions and laws**, **(c) statements about data, code, versions and reproducibility**. An issue can *invalidate* (a) or *contradict* (b) and (c). |
| "trace" | For each issue: identify the artifact it concerns, find every place in the paper that depends on that artifact, and give a verdict with page-level evidence. |

#### P1 — Make the frontier definition cumulative (Eq. 8 and its note)

- **Where:** p. 17, §6, Equation (8); p. 18, the box titled "Note on Equation (1)" directly after it.
- **Current text:**
  > Eq. (8): `F_A(H, p) = max{T : S_A(T,H) ≥ p}`
  >
  > Note box: "**Note on Equation (1)** — Equation (1) is the success-only primitive. §14.9 replaces it for scoring purposes with a delivered-outcome primitive S^net_A(T,H); the frontier F_A(H,p) is unchanged in form and is computed from the net surface. Equation (2) reads S_A(T,H) as non-increasing in T. Where a measured surface is not monotone, the frontier is taken cumulatively — the largest T such that every band up to T meets p — so that a pass at a hard band cannot certify a frontier above a band that fails. This is the same cumulative q* = min rule the I, O and SA achievement variables use."
- **Proposed text:**
  > Eq. (8): `F_A(H, p) = max{T_k : min_{j≤k} S_A(T_j, H) ≥ p}`
  >
  > Note box: "**Note on Equations (7) and (8)** — Equation (7) is the success-only primitive. §14.9 replaces it for scoring purposes with a delivered-outcome primitive S^net_A(T,H); the frontier keeps the form of Equation (8) and is computed from the net surface. Equation (8) is cumulative: a band counts only if every lower band also meets p, so a pass at a hard band cannot certify a frontier above a band that fails. When S_A(T,H) is non-increasing in T, it reduces to the largest T with S_A(T,H) ≥ p. This is the same cumulative q* = min rule the I, O and SA achievement variables use."
- **Why:**
  1. As printed, Eq. (8) defines the non-cumulative frontier, the exact rule of the defective public helper, and contradicts the paper's own cumulative rule (note box; §11.4 gate p. 24; addendum §5 p. 69) and `cumulative_policy.json`.
  2. A definition that is only correct when read together with a note invites the same implementation error in any reader's code.
  3. The note box cites "Equation (1)" and "Equation (2)", which are the memory and Individual certification laws (pp. 13–14), not the surface and frontier (Eqs. 7–8).
- **Effect on reported numbers:** none (no frontier is measured, Table C).

#### P2 — Make the §11.4 frontier formula cumulative and use one symbol

- **Where:** p. 23, §11.4 "Delegation: the (T, H) surface", first paragraph.
- **Current text:**
  > "Delegation has no ladder of the pair's own; it has a surface, and the "level" is the frontier `DF_h,p(A) = max{T : S_net(A,T,H ≤ h) ≥ p}`. The schema applies to each cell (b,h) of the surface."
- **Proposed text:**
  > "Delegation has no ladder of the pair's own; it has a surface, and the "level" is the cumulative frontier of Equation (8) computed on the net surface, `F_A(h,p) = max{T_k : min_{j≤k} S_net(A,T_j,H ≤ h) ≥ p}`. The schema applies to each cell (b,h) of the surface."

  And in the v2.6 addendum §5 (p. 69), replace the symbol `DF(h,p)` with `F_A(h,p)`, or add "(written F_A in §6)".
- **Why:**
  1. This is the second place the frontier is defined without lower-band retention. The gate on p. 24 then adds `K_T,<b|h`, so the section contradicts itself within one page.
  2. The paper uses three symbols for one quantity (`F_A` in §6, `DF_h,p` in §11.4, `DF(h,p)` in the addendum). Reviewers may read them as different quantities.
- **Effect on reported numbers:** none.

#### P3 — Correct what §14.2 says the achievement variables enforce

- **Where:** p. 29, §14.2 "Formal Definition and Meaning of the Symbols", fourth sentence.
- **Current text:**
  > "A_d ∈ [0,1] is a cumulative achievement variable for dimension d that already enforces lower-level retention."
- **Proposed text:**
  > "A_d ∈ [0,1] is the achievement variable for dimension d. For I, O and SA it enforces lower-level retention through Equation (14); for C, retention is applied within each cognitive domain before domain aggregation (Equation (13) and the v2.6 clarification); for DI, A_DI (Equations (15) and (17)) is a weighted mean over the delegation surface, and lower-band retention enters DI through the cumulative frontier F_A(H,p) of Equation (8), which must be reported beside it (§14.9)."
- **Alternative (not recommended now):** redefine A_DI with a `min_{j≤k}` term so it enforces retention itself. That would change every E1 number (Tables 37–40, Fig. 2) and require recomputation from the archive (DK-05).
- **Why:**
  1. The sentence is false for A_DI as defined: Eqs. (15) and (17) are plain weighted means over (T, H) cells with no retention term, unlike Eq. (14). A high-band success can offset a failed lower band.
  2. It is also not shown for A_C in the main text: Eq. (13) is a domain aggregation `G(R_general, …)`; the within-domain retention rule `q*_{C,k,d} = min_{j≤k} q_{C,j,d}` appears only in the v2.6 addendum §3 (p. 68).
  3. Every measured delegation number in the paper (E1) is an A_DI value, so readers need to know what property those numbers do and do not have. The proposed wording matches the paper's own instruction that "the scalar A_DI must never replace the frontier" (p. 32).
- **Effect on reported numbers:** none under the proposed text.

### 2.1 How we interpret it

| Phrase | Our interpretation |

| "where affected, make separately versioned corrections" | The fix goes in a **new file with its own version label**, never by editing the published one. Re-verified this task's findings by executing them, not by re-reading the record.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Regression suite, run in place | **15 tests, all pass, exit 0** |
| Mutation testing, done independently of the recorded run | **4 of 4 caught.** `q < p` → `q <= p`, `return frontier` → `return t`, `h_band[1:]` → `h_band[0:]`, `t += 1` → `t += 2` all make the suite fail; the module restored cleanly afterwards |
| The published `delegation_frontier` defect | **Real**, and worse than the publication notes say |
| `gp_gate` carrying the same class of defect | **Confirmed.** Its signature is `(metrics, thresholds)` — it receives no level and no lower-level results, so it cannot enforce GP retention even in principle |
| "No measured result affected" | **Confirmed**, and for a stronger reason than impact analysis — see below |

**The defect is worse than "takes the maximum individually passing band".** Run against the published scorer directly:

| Surface at H1, p = 0.80 | Published | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| T0 pass, T1 **fail**, T2 pass | 2 | 0 |
| T0 **fail**, T1 pass, T2 pass | **2** | None |

The second row is the sharp one: the published helper reports a frontier of T2 on a surface where the *easiest* band fails outright. It is not merely skipping a gap — it can certify a frontier for a system that passes nothing at the bottom.

**Why no measured result is affected turns out to be structural.** The paper's own Table 17 states the rule cumulatively — `DF_{h,p} = max{T_b : ∀j ≤ b, S_net(T_j, H ≤ h) ≥ p}` — with exactly the `∀j ≤ b` quantifier the code omits. So the manuscript is correct and the implementation contradicts it, rather than the other way round. And the single numeric frontier anywhere in the paper is hypothetical: "a system **may be** globally C4 with … a computer-use delegation frontier of T3/H1@p=0.80". There is no measured frontier value to be wrong.

That sharpens where the fix belongs: in the code, and in any scorer shipped beside the paper (S1), never in the manuscript's definition.
