## Sub-task 1 — Handoff

### 1.1 How we interpret it

The handoff is the deliverable that makes everything else findable. Nine tasks produced nine pull requests, each with its own record, scripts, logs and hashes — which is reviewable one at a time but gives the owner no single place to see what state the work is in or what he has to decide. One file fixes that.

"Clearly organized" means organized for the person who has to act on it, so the handoff is ordered by decision rather than by task number, and the blocker that unblocks the most comes first.

### 1.2 Results and analysis

`HANDOFF.md` carries the candidate hashes, every artifact's location, the status of all eleven tasks, the `not-applicable` items with their evidence, the six blockers in priority order, and the empty approval record for each author to fill in.

**The candidate hashes are recorded as provisional**, because they are. Every proposed correction across DK-02, DK-04, DK-06, DK-08 and DK-09 is unapplied and unapproved; approving any of them changes the PDF and therefore its hash. Recording a hash without saying that would imply a finality the candidate does not have.

**The blocker ordering is a finding in itself.** Written out in one list, the missing repository licence turns out to sit underneath the supplement rights question, the CC BY question and the archive question — one decision, three unblocked. That was not visible while each task looked at its own rights problem separately.

## A reporting requirement that was being skipped

The assignment asks for **one short update at the end of each working day**, in a fixed template, and for each task to carry a status from a fixed vocabulary — with the explicit rule that *"where no issue applies, provide evidence for `not-applicable` rather than silently skipping it."*

**Neither was being done — with one qualification that matters.** One message went to the owner, covering DK-01. After that, results went into pull requests with no daily summary.

The status vocabulary was not wholly unused: `tmlr-prep/tasks/README.md` was created on day 1 as a status index, with the right vocabulary and a row per task. It was then abandoned — stale on nine of eleven rows, still listing four completed tasks as "not yet written". Created and abandoned is a different failure from never attempted, and saying the stronger thing would have been inaccurate in my own favour by making the lapse look tidier than it was. The index is refreshed and now points at the handoff. The effect is the exact failure the rule exists to prevent: nine tasks reached `ready-for-review` without the owner being told, so decisions piled up instead of being grouped for minimal owner effort.

`status_update.md` records the update in the required template, applies the status vocabulary to all eleven tasks, and says plainly that earlier updates were not sent rather than backfilling them as though they had been. The four `not-applicable` items now carry evidence instead of being absent.

**Analysis.** This was the easiest requirement in the assignment to satisfy and the one most completely missed, and it was missed because it is not attached to any deliverable — no artifact is wrong without it, so nothing failed to flag it. Every other gap found in this work was caught by running something or reading something; this one was only catchable by re-reading the assignment's own process section, which is not part of any task's clause list.

## Two decisions that appeared in no task record

Found by reading `PAPER_SOURCE_HANDOVER.md` in full, which had only been read in part.

1. **The version number disagrees in three places.** The website PDF is v2.4 (2026-09-07), the release package is v2.6 (2026-09-06), and `build_arxiv.sh` writes `..._v2_4.pdf`. The lower number is the newer text: the build script and the release packaging were numbering independently, and the packaging counted the two addenda as 2.5 and 2.6. Nothing was rolled back. The handover recommends picking one number for the submission edition and changing the title page, the build filename, the website and the release label together — a recommendation, not a decision, and it had been carried nowhere.
2. **The title-page date — and here this record was wrong.** It said the candidate's title page reads September 3, 2026. It does not: the string appears **nowhere** in either TMLR edition. The date lived in the centred block `\maketitle` replaces, and the template has no date field for submissions, so it survives only in the source and any arXiv build. DK-03 had already recorded the drop correctly as decision B4; this record asserted the opposite from reading `main.tex` instead of the PDF, and attributed the point to DK-09, which never made it.

Neither blocks submission. Both make the title page wrong until decided, so both are now in the handoff's decision list.

## Review pass

The handoff's own numbers were the thing most likely to have gone stale, since every task record has been amended since it was written. They had.

**The per-task decision counts were wrong for seven of the ten tasks.** They were written once, by hand, and never re-derived:

| Task | Handoff said | Actually |
|---|---|---|
| DK-01 | 6 | **9** proposals A1–A9 |
| DK-03 | 5 | **6** (A5 plus B1–B5) |
| DK-04 | 5 | **6** substantive, drawn from a 17-item change log |
| DK-05 | "0 — findings only" | **5** numerical proposals C1–C5 |
| DK-06 | 5 | **12** (P1–P8, S1, N1 and related) |
| DK-07 | 6 | **7**, after this review added one |
| DK-09 | 5 | **6** revision proposals |

Only DK-02, DK-08 and DK-11 were right. The table now states the counting convention, because the records genuinely use different schemes — lettered proposals in four of them, numbered decision tables in four, a change log in DK-04 — and a single unexplained integer per row hid that.

This is the failure the handoff exists to prevent, occurring inside the handoff: a summary that stops tracking what it summarises is worse than no summary, because it is read instead of the records rather than alongside them.
